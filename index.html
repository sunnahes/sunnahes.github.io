Upcoming new moons (geocentric conjunction)

Traditionally the Islamic month begins with the first sighting of the hilal on the evening after the conjunction (ruʾyah), or by calculation depending on the country. Use “View” and step through days to find the first visible evening at your location.

Methodology, glossary and sources

Glossary

  • Conjunction (geocentric new moon): the instant when the ecliptic longitudes of the Sun and the Moon coincide. Not observable.
  • Moon age: time elapsed since conjunction.
  • LAG: minutes between sunset and moonset. If ≤ 0, the hilal is impossible.
  • Best time (Tb): Tb = Tsunset + 4/9·LAG (Yallop 1997).
  • ARCL (elongation): arc of light, angular separation Moon–Sun.
  • ARCV: arc of vision, difference in altitude Moon–Sun (without refraction).
  • DAZ: difference in azimuth Sun–Moon.
  • W′: crescent width in arcminutes: W′ = SD′·(1 − cos ARCL).
  • q (Yallop): q = [ARCV − (11.8371 − 6.3226·W′ + 0.7319·W′² − 0.1018·W′³)]/10. Categories A–F.
  • V (Odeh/ICOP): V = ARCV − (7.1651 − 6.3226·W + 0.7319·W² − 0.1018·W³). Zones A–D.
  • Danjon limit: with elongation < ~7° the crescent does not form and is physically unobservable.

Calculation conventions

  • Ephemerides: Astronomy Engine (VSOP87 + truncated ELP2000-82, ~1′ accuracy).
  • Yallop’s q: geocentric ARCV (topocentric altitude corrected for parallax) and topocentric W′, evaluated at Tb, as in NAO TN 69.
  • Odeh’s V: topocentric ARCV and W, evaluated at Tb.
  • Sun/moon sets with standard refraction (−0.8333°, upper limb).
  • Displayed altitudes use normal refraction; criterion altitudes do not.
  • Actual visibility also depends on atmospheric extinction, dust, humidity and the local horizon: these criteria are statistical (based on hundreds of real observations).
  • Umm al-Qura and tabular calendars via ICU (Intl). The Islamic day begins at maghrib.
